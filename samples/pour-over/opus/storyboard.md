# Storyboard: "Your first V60 pour-over" (30 s, EN + KO)

**Style: warm café bench.** Cream kraft-paper background (#F4ECE0) with light grain; espresso-brown ink (#2B1E16) and a warm grey-brown `dim` (#7A6756). `acc` is coffee-cherry terracotta (#C4553A), `alt` is water blue (#4F8CA6), `bad` is honey amber (#D89A2B). A wooden counter runs along the bottom. There's no frame, just a small five-segment "STEP n OF 5" pill on the step scenes. Type is rounded: Nunito 800/900 for Latin and Jua for Hangul. Jua has only one weight, so Korean is set at 400 and never faux-bolded. The scale readouts use the mono font. Headlines `rise`, and each cut's wipe clears the old copy and uncovers the new copy in one pass. Objects move on springs (`SPRING.heavy` for large ones, `snappy` for readouts and tags). Cuts come in two kinds from the same world. On `rinse`, `dose` and `done`, a coffee-brown wave sweeps the copy column left to right. On the pouring steps (`bloom`, `pour`) and `close`, a stream-coloured edge pours down the copy column. Music is the `soft` groove at 104 BPM, a Fmaj7–Em7–Dm7–Cmaj7 loop, with no sci-fi sounds. **Why:** a café's in-store screen for complete beginners should feel like a friendly barista at the bar, not a tech demo. Light and warm matches a daytime café. Rounded type reads as friendly. It's a calm, unhurried how-to, so `rise`/`soft` fit better than `slam`/`electro`.

**Metaphor: one brew bar, seen side-on the whole time.** A single illustration stays centre-right for the whole film: a V60 drawn as a cutaway, on a glass server, on a scale whose LCD shows **grams and a timer**, with a gooseneck kettle resting on the counter at the right. It changes state from scene to scene: filter in, grounds, bloom dome, rising water, drips slowing. Each number appears where a real brewer would read it, on the scale. The camera pushes in close on the bloom (×1.4) and pulls back for the pour. The copy column on the left changes with each step. The film ends on the café's **recipe card**; after one full viewing the last frame is the poster. The metaphor lives in the objects. Copy stays plain.

Tempo 104 BPM (1 beat = 0.577 s) · 52 beats = **30.0 s** · 7 scenes · layout: copy column x 110–890, brew bar centred on x 1340. The first-visit poster (t = 3.1 s) keeps canvas y ≈ 410–680 on the left free for the play button.

## Pace and reading checks

- **Hook by 2 s:** the brew bar is on screen at frame 0, the dripper lands by ~0.5 s, and the headline is readable at ~0.6 s.
- **Something new every 2–4 s:** every scene has a mid-scene state change.
  - `open` lights the LCD, then pops the five step dots.
  - `rinse` folds and seats the filter, pours, then tips the server out.
  - `dose` pours and counts, levels the bed, then tares.
  - `bloom` pours, then pushes in on the dome while the ring runs.
  - `pour` pulls back and draws the spiral while the counter climbs.
  - `done` stops the timer, then lifts the dripper.
  - `close` pours into the mug, then fills the card row by row.
- **Reading time** (EN words × 0.3 + 1 s; KO characters × 0.08 + 1 s), shown as needed / available:
  - `rinse`: 4.0 / 4.0 s
  - `dose`: 4.3 / 4.6 s
  - `bloom`: 4.3 / 4.6 s
  - `pour`: 5.2 / 5.2 s
  - `done`: 4.3 / 4.0 s (loops on the café screen)
  - `close`: the card holds on the last frame
  - Korean lines are all ≤ 3.4 s. `rinse` and `pour` are right at their limit, and the film loops on a café screen, so a second viewing covers anyone who misses a line.
- Every number on screen is in `facts.md`. The timer is marked as sped up.

## Changes from the approved storyboard (made during the build and the critique loop)

- **Instructions moved into the body copy.**
  - Critique round 2 found three meanings only in small tags. "Then zero the scale." and "Aim for 2:30–3:00" are now body lines.
  - `done` grew from 6 to 7 beats and `close` shrank from 8 to 7, so the total is still 52 beats (30.0 s).
  - The counter pills are 44 px bold.
- **Cuts.** The wipe redraws the previous scene's copy, frozen, in the part it hasn't uncovered yet, so there are no text-less frames at a cut.
- **Body copy is 58 px, and long lines now take two lines.**
  - Critique round 1 found the Korean body lines unreadable at phone size.
  - Headline and sub wording are unchanged, except for the three Korean sub lines split into two lines each.
  - The Korean pour sub line is now "가운데부터 시작해서 / 종이에는 닿지 않게".
- **Recipe card rows.** Grind and temperature moved into the bold value line, e.g. "22 g · medium-fine" and "400 g · 93 °C", so they read at phone size.
- **Sped-up tag.** It moved from under the ring to a pill under the scale, and stays from the first pour until the end of step 5.
- **Opening.** The title moved up out of the play button's band on the poster. Five numbered step dots were added as the opening's second beat, since round 1 found no change after 1 s.
- **Camera and cuts.**
  - The camera now pushes in to a ×1.4 close-up on the bloom and pulls back for the pour.
  - There's a second cut style, a pour from above, for `bloom`, `pour` and `close`.
  - The kettle stays on screen in `dose`.
  - Copy, tags and the stepper leave in the last 0.25 s before each cut instead of vanishing at it.
- **Close.** Re-staged after critique round 3.
  - The mug is large and sits under the headline.
  - The server pours into it, and the scale and kettle bow out.
  - The recipe card pops in (instead of sliding) only after the rig has gone, and its rows start right after it lands.
- **93 °C** is a pill at a fixed spot left of the stream, shown while water is poured. It is no longer hung on the kettle, where it could hide behind the body.
- **Timer.** It stops at 2:45, an illustrative reading inside the 2:30–3:00 window, as recorded in `facts.md`.

## Build notes

- `CFG.cta.href = ''`, `endCta: null`. The close is only a recipe card.
- Languages: `I18N.en` (default) and `I18N.ko` (`?lang=ko`).
- Music: `groove: 'soft'`, `bpm: 104`. `liteScenes: ['dose','bloom']` keeps the bed quieter while viewers read numbers. `arpScenes: ['pour']`. `tailBeats: 6`. The opening scene carries its own pad and bass, because the groove starts in scene 2.
- Café screens often run muted, so every step must read without sound. The sound only adds warmth.
